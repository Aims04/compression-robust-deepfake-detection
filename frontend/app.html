Compression-Robust Deepfake Detection

Research project · DFDC sample, 391 videos

Video shared online is re-encoded and downscaled, which removes the fine traces many deepfake detectors depend on. This project measures how much detection accuracy is lost to compression, tests whether training on compressed copies recovers it, and studies whether the detector can abstain when it cannot tell, instead of guessing.

Results

The same videos were re-encoded at increasing compression and scored by two detectors: one trained on clean video only, and one trained on clean plus compressed copies. A detector trained only on clean video falls toward chance under heavy compression; compression-aware training recovers much of the loss without hurting clean accuracy.

Method

  1. Faces. A face detector (YuNet) finds the largest face in 16 evenly spaced frames and crops it.
  2. Features. A frozen CLIP ViT-B/16 vision model embeds each crop. Embeddings are pooled over frames as mean and standard deviation.
  3. Classifier. Five logistic regressions trained on bootstrap samples of the training videos vote. Their output is calibrated with temperature scaling.
  4. Abstention. If calibrated confidence is below 65%, the answer is “not sure”. On clean video this raises balanced accuracy from 0.76 to 0.83 when the model answers only its 50% most confident cases.

Scope and next steps

  • Sample size. The study uses 391 DFDC videos (77 real), so the results describe trends with wide intervals. Adding real footage is the first extension.
  • Single dataset. Models are trained and tested within DFDC. The next experiment is cross-dataset evaluation on Celeb-DF and FaceForensics++, and on newer generators.
  • Simulated compression. Re-encoding uses ffmpeg settings, not downloads from real platforms. Comparing against platform re-encodes would test how well the simulation transfers.
  • Use. Outputs are model estimates for research, not forensic evidence.
